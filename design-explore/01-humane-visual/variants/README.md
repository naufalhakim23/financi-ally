# Variants: humane visual pass

Three interpretations of the same brief (warm but calm, accent hue added, softer/rounder/airier,
category color presence, expressive type, texture on emptiness). Same content everywhere; only
the visual treatment moves.

## variant-a-morning-air — warm indigo, Headspace-leaning

The softest read. Cream-tinted warm off-whites replace the cool slate ramp; periwinkle-indigo
accent used gently (FAB, tab-active, one hero moment). Airiest density, largest radii (cards
24-28px), greeting set in display type, empty states built from layered tinted tiles. The
question it answers: how calm can this get before precision suffers?

## variant-b-sea-glass — teal, Stripe-leaning

Polish over softness. Warm-gray neutrals (less cream than A), crisp hairlines stay from v1.3,
teal accent applied with Stripe restraint: accent as punctuation, not paint. Balanced density,
radii grow only one step from today. Hero gets a barely-there tonal gradient (light, not
decoration). The question: can warmth come almost entirely from tuning, not rework?

## variant-c-plum-hearth — plum/mulberry, boldest

The accent leads. Plum carries FAB, tab-active, hero tint washes and the greeting moment;
category tints get their strongest presence (tinted rows in budgets/reports, tinted donut
legend chips). Density stays balanced but type hierarchy is the most expressive: big warm
greeting, big hero numeral. Still calm: no gradients-as-decoration, semantic colors untouched.
The question: how much personality fits inside "calm and trustworthy"?

## Comparison rubric

Flip between the same page across variants and ask, in order:

1. Gut: which one would you open at 11pm to log a coffee without it feeling like work?
2. Trust: does the accent make money feel less serious anywhere? (That's a fail.)
3. Hue: which accent family sits right next to the semantic green/red/amber?
4. Air: is A's spaciousness luxury or waste on the Reports page?
5. Emptiness: which empty-state treatment makes "nothing here yet" feel inviting, not abandoned?
6. Steal list: elements you'd pull from a losing variant into the winner.
